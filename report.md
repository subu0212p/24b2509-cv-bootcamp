# CIFAR-10N (worst) – report
All accuracies are percentages on the 5,000 validation images. Clean labels were used only for measurement.

**Q1.** Noise concentrates in visually similar pairs: automobile↔truck (2,373 wrong labels, 13.1% of all 18,109), cat↔dog (1,758; 9.7%), deer↔horse (916, mostly deer→horse), airplane↔ship (748) and deer→dog (491). These five give ~35% of the noise; the rest is a diffuse background (e.g. many images wrongly called "bird"). Per true class, cat (51.5%) and deer (51.4%) are mislabeled most, horse (28.0%) and ship (31.6%) least. Unlike the uniform off-diagonal of the synthetic labels, human noise is asymmetric and image-dependent.

**Q2.** Around epoch 34. Training accuracy on mislabeled images (agreement with their wrong label) stays flat at 12–15% for epochs 1–33, then climbs from epoch 34 (14.7%) to 23% at epoch 44 and 69.6% at epoch 60, as the cosine learning rate shrinks. Simultaneously, clean validation accuracy peaks (81.0%, epochs 33/35) and falls to 72.1%, and the share of mislabeled training images predicted as their true class drops from 70% (epoch 33) to 20%. Noisy validation accuracy peaks at epoch 44 (55.84%), our early-stopping epoch.

**Q3.** Checked against clean labels (measurement only): the DivideMix ensemble's 1−p(label) was the best single signal (AUROC 0.967, precision@k 0.906), then run-averaged CE margin (0.965) and 4-fold out-of-fold confidence (0.938); final-epoch CE loss was worst (0.745) because memorized wrong labels have low loss. Our submitted rank-average of CV and DivideMix scores reaches AUROC 0.971, precision@k 0.893. Failure mode: systematic human confusions are learned as genuine patterns, so those wrong labels look plausible – only 58% of cat→dog and 63% of deer→horse flips are caught, while hard but correctly labeled cats get flagged (18%).

**Q4.** Best by noisy validation was CV filter+relabel (57.48%; DivideMix 57.22%, GCE 56.96%, early-stopped CE 55.84%, final CE 51.68%). It helped because retraining only on samples whose label agrees with an out-of-fold model (55.6% kept, 92.2% correct when measured) removes most wrong labels before they can be memorized. But its lead is ~13 images, and noisy validation rewards reproducing consistent human errors that the filter keeps. Measured clean accuracy ranks DivideMix far ahead (88.2% vs 83.5%): it reuses discarded images as unlabeled data, co-divides with two networks and resists memorization through mixup.

**Q5.** Yes. With synthetic noise (selected on synthetic validation): DivideMix 53.20% > GCE 52.84% > filter 51.46% > early-stopped CE 50.18% > final CE 45.16%; with real noise filter ranked first and GCE last among robust methods. Symmetric wrong labels are random and mutually inconsistent, so they keep high loss and separate easily; GCE, designed for symmetric noise, gains most (clean 81.9%→88.8%), and the noisy-validation ranking matches the clean ranking exactly. Human noise is structured and plausible, so networks learn it as a pattern, defeating loss-based separation and making noisy-validation selection unreliable.
